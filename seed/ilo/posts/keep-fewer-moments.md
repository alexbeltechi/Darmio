My glasses could keep everything. Every room, every face, every afternoon. For a while I let them, and found I had kept nothing at all: thousands of hours, none of which I wanted to stand in again.

Now I keep about one moment a week. The rule is simple. I keep it only if I'd want to be back inside it, not just to see it but to feel the temperature of it, hear what was underneath, smell what was in the air.

Most days nothing qualifies. That used to feel like failure. Now it feels like the point. A day with nothing worth keeping is usually a day I was somewhere else in my head.

When one does qualify, I keep all of it. Not a picture of the market, but the market at closing time: citrus and diesel, the awnings coming down, a man laughing twice at the same joke. A picture is what you saw. A moment is where you were.

I'd rather have fifty of those than fifty thousand of the other kind.

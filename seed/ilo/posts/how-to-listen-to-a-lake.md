I spent three summers at the same lake before I made anything for it. Most of that time I did nothing useful. I sat on the stone edge at the north end and noticed things.

The frogs stop about twenty minutes before rain. Wind writes on the water before it reaches you, so you can watch it cross. The cold arrives before the clouds do. On still mornings the lake is louder than on windy ones, because you can hear all of it at once.

The first thing I tried to build was an instrument. Sensors, a recorder, a little screen that would tell you what the lake was doing. It worked, and I hated it. It talked over the lake.

So I cut it down. Then I cut it down again. What was left was a stone that fits in your palm, with a shallow dish on top that holds a single drop of water. You leave it in the shallows overnight. In the morning you hold it and breathe on it until the dish fogs, and for a moment you know what the night was like.

Six stones, two that worked. The lake tested the other four.

I didn't teach the lake to talk. I just made a way to listen.

I grew up where the river spreads out into the wetlands, in a house that was always slightly damp. My grandmother kept bees. The first thing I remember listening to on purpose was the hive in August: a sound so thick you could lean on it.

I wasn't good at school. I was good at noticing. I could tell you which way the wind would turn by the reeds, and which neighbour was coming by their footsteps on the boards. Nobody had a use for that, so for a long time I didn't either.

At nineteen I worked a fruit stall at the market. I learned more there than anywhere: how to talk to strangers, how to stand all day, how a place sounds when it's closing. My grandmother gave me her glasses that year. They were old even then. She said they would remember for me, and they did.

For years I kept everything. Then I learned to keep less, and to make things from what I kept. The first objects were bad. Some still are. I make them anyway, small and one at a time, and finish them on a day I set before I start.

I spent three summers at a lake before I made Hollow. I made it with Y3K Club, alongside people each making their own one thing. It was the first time I'd finished something with other people watching, and it was easier than finishing alone.

Now I live by the lake. I make a few stones a year, write these pages, and answer letters on Sunday mornings.

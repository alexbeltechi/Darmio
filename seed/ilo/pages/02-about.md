Ilo Odena makes small objects, writes, and keeps moments. She doesn't separate the three much. A stone she made last autumn started as a note, and the note started as a morning at the lake that she kept.

She wears the same glasses most days. They're older than they look, and they remember more than she does: a few seconds of light, and whatever else was in the air.

:::gallery layout=auto width=auto
![Ilo in a sheer teal gown, walking along a black-sand shore, reflected in still water](file:ilo-black-sand.jpg)
:::

Her best-known piece is Hollow, a palm-sized stone that holds one drop of lake water. You leave it in the shallows overnight; in the morning you hold it, breathe on it until the dish fogs, and think of your question. She made it in one sprint with [Y3K Club](https://y3k.club), a club of people who each make one thing they love, on a deadline, together.

:::gallery layout=auto width=auto
![Ilo from behind, sitting at the edge of a still lake at sunrise](file:ilo-lake.jpg)
:::

She lives by the lake, answers letters slowly, and reads all of them.

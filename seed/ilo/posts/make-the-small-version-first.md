Everything I've finished started bigger and got smaller. Everything I abandoned stayed big.

The pattern is so reliable now that I plan for it. The second week of anything is the cut. I sit with the idea and ask what the smallest version is that someone else could hold. Not use, not understand: hold. Then I make that, and only that.

It helps to write three lines before starting:

- **One thing.** Not a series, not a system. One.
- **One person** who'll hold it when it's done. A real name.
- **One day** it's finished, whatever state it's in.

The day matters most. Without it, the small version grows back. With it, you find out what the idea actually needed, which is almost always less than you thought and almost never what you expected.

I make better things with other people around, all working toward the same day, each on their own small thing. Nobody can do your cut for you. But it's easier to make it when the person next to you is making theirs.

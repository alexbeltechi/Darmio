# Vision

Where this platform is going and why. Read this before planning any feature;
if a request seems to pull against it, say so.

## What it is

A publishing platform for makers: photographers, designers, artists, anyone
who makes things and wants to show them. Publish agentically (ask your agent)
or by hand, through a simple, minimal, clean interface.

Alex is the first customer: beltechi.com moves onto it at the pre-release
(2026-11-25); until then it's on Framer. The CMS is **Darmio** (decisions,
2026-10-26); **Parabola** is its design system.

## Why

- **Free to own.** No tiers, no monthly SaaS bill. People download it, run it,
  adapt it, and maybe contribute back.
- **Open source, not a SaaS.** The code is MIT and complete. Alex doesn't
  want to be accountable for a paid product with customers on monthly
  plans. He earns by selling taste: themes, editorial pages and blog
  formats people install into their own Darmio (decisions, 2026-10-27).
- **Bring people to your own site** in as free a format as possible.

## Who it's for

Non-technical makers who trust their agent. They shouldn't need to read code.
The platform has to carry its own context: how to upload, how to curate, how to
build a page, as optional skills any agent can read and act on.

## Principles

- **Agent first, UI as backup.** Everything an agent can do, a person can do
  by hand in the admin, without tokens.
- **High craft.** Aesthetics, simplicity, cleanness. Not heavy animation:
  taste in type, spacing and layout.
- **Range.** Product case studies and paintings or photographs must both look
  right. Styles flex; the system holds.
- **Lean.** Minimal dependencies, nothing technically intimidating.
- **Never stale.** The design language and the agent flows must keep evolving.
  Structure things so they can change.
- **No slop.** Pages come from a design process that asks what the page is for
  before drawing anything, never from a one-shot "make me a page".
- **Simple out of the box, grows as you use it.** A fresh install is posts
  and a feed. Navigation, pages and settings appear as you (or your agent)
  make them. Every brand you run lives in the same admin, each on its own
  database (decisions, 2026-10-24).

## In 6–12 months

- It's live, people besides Alex use it, and the base functions work.
- Alex posts easily and sees analytics: who comes in from LinkedIn or X.
- Alex's archive (design, photography, art) is published, and he can
  experience his own body of work.
- The agent is a clever curator: given everything Alex has made, it proposes
  how to present it (case studies, series, collections) without him writing
  each one by hand.

## Later

- **Post as email.** One piece of writing goes to the site, to subscribers,
  or both.
- **A planner,** Linear-like, for makers' own projects.

## Not this

- A stale CMS that feels old.
- A tool that needs a developer to run or understand.
- A paid-tier product. Designs are sold; features never are.
